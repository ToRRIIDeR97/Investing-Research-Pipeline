## 1. Executive Decision Summary

MercadoLibre is one of the strongest integrated commerce-and-fintech platforms in Latin America. Its marketplace liquidity, fulfillment density, payment network, consumer and merchant credit, brand, data and loyalty systems reinforce one another. The business still has a long runway because both online retail and formal financial-services penetration remain below mature-market levels.

The investment conclusion is less favorable than the business-quality conclusion.

At roughly **$1,835 per share**, the stock is close to the central estimate of current intrinsic value:

| Valuation output | Value |
|---|---:|
| Base fair value | **$1,832/share** |
| Probability-weighted fair value | **$1,848/share** |
| Bear/conservative value | **$859/share** |
| Bull/aggressive value | **$3,236/share** |
| Base upside/downside | approximately **0%** |

The current price therefore appears to embed a credible base outcome rather than a distressed or obviously excessive outcome. The price is approximately justified if MercadoLibre can achieve by 2035:

- Revenue near **$129 billion**
- Operating margin near **15.2%**
- Gross loans near **$67 billion**
- Latin American online-retail penetration near **28%**
- MercadoLibre commerce share near **35%**
- Continuing logistics, payment and credit reinvestment without material dilution

The central unresolved question is:

> **Are free shipping, fulfillment expansion and credit-card growth temporarily suppressing margins while strengthening the moat, or are they revealing structurally lower incremental returns?**

Q2 2026 supports the first half of the thesis—demand remained extremely strong—but does not yet prove the payback. Revenue grew 50%, foreign-exchange-neutral GMV grew 36%, acquiring TPV grew 42% and dual commerce-plus-fintech users grew 37%. At the same time, EBIT fell 17%, EBIT margin declined to 6.7%, and net income declined 11%.

The business can perform well while the stock produces only an ordinary required return. A more attractive setup would require at least one of:

1. A lower price that creates a margin of safety;
2. Evidence that shipping investment raises frequency and route density enough to restore margins;
3. Evidence that credit-card cohorts generate durable risk-adjusted returns after provisions, funding and required equity capital;
4. A sustainably higher long-term monetization or margin path than the current base case.

## 3. Asset Definition

A MercadoLibre common share is a residual claim on the future distributable economics of an integrated Latin American platform that connects:

- Buyers and sellers;
- Merchants and payment customers;
- Consumers and credit;
- Logistics capacity and marketplace orders;
- Advertising demand and transaction intent;
- Loyalty benefits and cross-platform behavior.

Value accrues to shareholders through:

1. Growth in commerce GMV and monetization;
2. Growth in payment volume and financial-services revenue;
3. Risk-adjusted credit income;
4. Operating leverage after investment cycles;
5. High incremental returns on software, data, logistics density and distribution;
6. Limited share dilution;
7. Potential future capital returns after growth investment moderates.

The investor does **not** own a simple retailer or a conventional bank. A standard enterprise-value bridge is misleading because customer funds, restricted cash, loans and financial liabilities are integral to Mercado Pago's operations. The valuation therefore uses adjusted equity cash flow:

```text
Adjusted equity cash flow
= NOPAT
+ depreciation and amortization
− capital expenditure
− change in core operating working capital
− equity capital required for incremental credit growth
```

## 5. Customer Economics and Decision Process

### Buyers

**Problem:** Find broad local assortment, competitive prices, reliable delivery, buyer protection, payments and credit in one place.

**Economic benefit:**

```text
Buyer value
= price and assortment
+ time saved
+ delivery reliability
+ buyer protection
+ payment convenience
+ access to credit
− shipping cost
− switching and search cost
```

The purchase is usually discretionary at the transaction level but the platform becomes habitual when selection, trust and delivery are consistently better than alternatives.

**Reasons to delay or switch:**

- Better price or subsidy elsewhere;
- Longer delivery time;
- Lower trust;
- Weak product availability;
- Credit denial or high financing cost;
- Reduced shipping benefits.

### Sellers and brands

**Problem:** Reach buyers, convert traffic, process payments, fulfill orders, advertise and finance working capital.

**Economic benefit:**

```text
Seller ROI
= incremental gross profit from MercadoLibre demand
− marketplace fees
− advertising
− fulfillment and shipping
− returns
− financing cost
− inventory and operational complexity
```

Sellers can multi-home. MercadoLibre's moat therefore cannot rest on exclusivity; it must continually deliver superior conversion and economics.

### Payment merchants

Merchants choose Mercado Pago when it increases conversion, provides convenient acquiring, integrates software and settlement, or offers working-capital products. The primary constraints are merchant pricing, Pix, bank competition, regulatory requirements and service quality.

### Credit users

Credit demand can grow much faster than safe monetizable demand. The required conversion chain is:

```text
Eligible user
→ approved user
→ originated balance
→ active and profitable balance
→ collected interest and fees
→ realized losses
→ funding cost
→ regulatory and equity capital
→ risk-adjusted shareholder return
```

The loan book is therefore not itself proof of value creation.

## 8. Principal Value Drivers

### 1. Commerce GMV, penetration and share

```text
MercadoLibre GMV
= Latin American retail market
× online penetration
× MercadoLibre share of online retail
```

The base case assumes online penetration reaches 28% and MercadoLibre maintains or increases share to 35% by 2035.

**Failure condition:** GMV growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters while peers continue gaining.

### 2. Commerce monetization

Commerce revenue depends on marketplace fees, logistics, product sales, ads and subscriptions. The historical model implies commerce revenue around 24–25% of GMV, but this is a blended accounting measure, not a pure marketplace take rate.

**Failure condition:** Monetization requires seller economics to deteriorate or creates share loss.

### 3. Payment volume and active financial users

```text
Non-credit fintech revenue
= TPV
× blended payment and financial-services monetization
```

Acquiring TPV is especially important because it proves Mercado Pago has value beyond MercadoLibre checkout.

**Failure condition:** Acquiring growth falls below 20% while fintech competitors maintain momentum.

### 4. Credit risk-adjusted economics

```text
Credit contribution
= average loans
× risk-adjusted yield
− funding and servicing
− required equity capital charge
```

The model deducts 20% of incremental gross loans as required equity capital. This is an analytical assumption rather than disclosed regulatory capital.

**Failure condition:** Delinquency exceeds 9%, loss-adjusted margin falls below 14%, or credit grows above 60% while cohort quality deteriorates.

### 5. Operating-margin payback

Margin recovery is the most valuation-sensitive driver. At the reference price, the model requires a long-term operating margin around 15.2%. If sustainable margin reaches only 12%, approximately **$168 billion** of 2035 revenue would be required to justify the same price under the model.

**Failure condition:** Operating margin remains below 6% through 2027 without measurable improvements in frequency, logistics unit cost or credit economics.

## 10. Moat Proof and Attack

### Moat conclusion

**Overall moat:** **Strong, approximately 8.3/10**  
**Confidence:** Moderate-high  
**State:** Operationally strengthening, financially unproven at the current investment intensity

| Moat | Strength / 5 | Evidence | Main attack | Residual moat after attack |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Marketplace liquidity | 4.3 | Buyer traffic, seller assortment and GMV growth | Amazon, Shopee and Temu subsidize price and sellers | Strong local liquidity in core markets |
| Logistics density | 4.4 | Fulfillment reach, delivery speed and conversion | Competitors build networks or subsidize carriers | Density and merchant integration remain difficult to copy |
| Commerce-fintech integration | 4.5 | Dual-platform users grew 37% | Nubank, banks and Pix unbundle payments and credit | Cross-use remains valuable even without exclusivity |
| Transaction-data underwriting | 3.6 | Commerce and payment behavior informs credit | Open finance and banks use broader data and cheaper funding | Better for thin-file and ecosystem-active users |
| Brand and trust | 4.0 | Recognition, buyer protection and continuing user growth | Fraud, service failure or superior rival experience | Strong but maintenance-dependent |

### Red-team attacks

#### Attack 1: A direct commerce competitor cuts effective prices by 20–30%

**Likely outcome:** Moat survives partially.

MercadoLibre can defend through assortment, fulfillment, seller tools, buyer protection and ecosystem benefits, but price-sensitive customers can multi-home. Defense raises shipping and marketing costs, lowering near-term margins.

#### Attack 2: Nubank, banks and Pix separate fintech from commerce

**Likely outcome:** Fintech moat survives partially, not fully.

Payments and credit have lower switching costs than marketplace logistics. The strongest defense is cross-platform use, merchant acquiring and transaction-level data. The weakest segment is financially sophisticated prime users who can obtain cheaper products elsewhere.

#### Attack 3: Sellers adopt multichannel software and distribute everywhere

**Likely outcome:** Marketplace exclusivity weakens but liquidity remains.

MercadoLibre does not need exclusive sellers. It needs to deliver enough incremental conversion and fulfillment efficiency that sellers continue allocating inventory, ads and service spending to the platform.

#### Attack 4: Credit losses normalize above modeled levels

**Likely outcome:** Credit economics and valuation fall materially, while the commerce moat survives.

Credit is an amplifier of the ecosystem, not the only moat. A credit failure would reduce fintech profit, consume capital and weaken loyalty, but would not eliminate marketplace liquidity or logistics density.

#### Attack 5: Regulation forces portability, lower pricing or higher capital

**Likely outcome:** Payment and credit economics weaken; scale becomes more valuable but returns fall.

### No-moat counterfactual

Without the integrated moat, a plausible business would have:

- Lower commerce share, perhaps 20–25% rather than 35%;
- Higher customer acquisition and shipping cost;
- Lower seller monetization;
- Less advertising attachment;
- Lower payment and credit cross-use;
- Operating margin closer to 8–10%;
- Lower incremental ROIC;
- Valuation economics broadly resembling the **$850–$1,100** bear range.

The current price therefore depends materially on the moat remaining intact and margin payback eventually appearing.

At approximately $1,835 per share, the market is not pricing in perfection, but it is pricing in successful reinvestment.

| Implied variable | Market-implied outcome | Classification |
|---|---:|---|
| 2035 revenue | About $129bn | Demanding but plausible |
| 2035 operating margin | About 15.2% | Requires material recovery |
| 2035 gross loans | About $67bn | Plausible only with stable loss economics |
| 2035 online-retail penetration | About 28% | Plausible |
| MercadoLibre online-commerce share | About 35% | Demanding but defensible |
| Required 2035 revenue if margin is only 12% | About $168bn | Operationally demanding |

The current valuation therefore requires more than strong demand. It requires:

```text
Strong demand
+ stable or rising share
+ safe credit growth
+ logistics payback
+ margin recovery
+ limited dilution
```

## 17. Expected Return and Permanent-Loss Risk

A fair value close to the current price does **not** imply a near-zero long-term return. If the base assumptions occur, the investor should approximately earn the model's 11% required return through the realization of future cash flows and value growth.

It does mean there is little **extra** expected return from buying below estimated intrinsic value.

### Outcome interpretation

| Outcome | Present-value range | Investment implication |
|---|---:|---|
| Bear operations | $859–$1,429 | Material permanent-loss risk |
| Base operations | $1,418–$2,373 | Current price near central value |
| Bull operations | $1,929–$3,236 | Large upside if margin and credit economics outperform |

### Main permanent-loss mechanisms

1. Structural operating-margin decline;
2. Credit losses and funding needs that exceed risk-adjusted yield;
3. Long-term share loss to subsidized competitors;
4. Regulation that lowers payment or credit economics;
5. Capital intensity that remains higher than modeled;
6. Management transition or capital-allocation failure;
7. Currency and macroeconomic shocks that damage both demand and credit.

Balance-sheet failure is not the base risk. The more likely risk is that a durable business produces lower shareholder economics than the current price requires.

## 21. Final Decision

# WATCHLIST

MercadoLibre has:

- Durable and mutually reinforcing commerce and fintech economics;
- Strong customer value;
- A proven ability to grow across multiple product layers;
- A strong, attack-resistant but maintenance-intensive moat;
- Limited dilution;
- A credible long-term runway.

The stock does **not** currently offer a clear margin of safety under the central model.

The base valuation of approximately **$1,832 per share** is nearly equal to the reference price. A current buyer is primarily underwriting the successful conversion of today's margin sacrifice into future logistics density, credit-card engagement and higher risk-adjusted cash flow.

The most important future evidence is not another quarter of high revenue growth. It is:

1. Stabilization and recovery of operating margin;
2. Healthy credit-card cohort performance;
3. Continued growth in dual-platform users;
4. Improving logistics economics;
5. Stable per-share ownership.

A model-derived watch zone around **$1,400–$1,500** would provide more protection under base operations with conservative valuation assumptions, but it is not a guaranteed floor or a personalized purchase recommendation.
