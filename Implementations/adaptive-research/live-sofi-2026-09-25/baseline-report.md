## 2. Company genesis and strategic identity

**Strategic Identity**: SoFi created university-specific funds for alumni to invest in and used the proceeds to make student loans ranging from $5,000 to $200,000. The loans carried a 6.24% fixed rate that could drop to 5.99%, lower than federal Stafford and PLUS loans and many private loans. It later expanded to in-school and consolidation loans. SoFi's early student-lending mission evolved into a member-centric financial services platform spanning borrowing, saving, spending, investing, and protection.

The no-moat case assumes roughly 8% net margin, 5% revenue growth, and 12% reinvestment of revenue to defend the franchise.

## 20. Risks and catalysts

#### Risks:
- **Regulatory capital constraint** (Severity: 5): SoFi operates as a bank holding company and must maintain regulatory capital ratios while growing loans and deposits. If Tier 1 capital falls toward minimum plus buffers, management may need to slow balance-sheet growth, retain earnings, sell loans, or raise capital, reducing ROE and terminal book value.
- **Shareholder dilution or equity issuance** (Severity: 4): If SoFi needs equity to fund growth, satisfy regulatory capital, or offset credit losses, it may issue shares beyond the reported diluted count of 1,351.99 million. A higher share count divides terminal equity and earnings over more shares, reducing per-share returns.
- **Funding and capital markets dependence** (Severity: 4): SoFi's earnings depend on loan sale prices and securitization/warehouse financing. Disruptions reduce gain-on-sale, liquidity, and origination capacity, potentially forcing more balance-sheet retention and capital usage.
- **Deposit funding and net interest margin pressure** (Severity: 4): An economic downturn or intensified deposit competition can cause deposit outflows, lower average checking and savings balances, higher funding costs, and greater reliance on wholesale funding. This can compress net interest margin and reduce net interest income.
- **Interest rate risk** (Severity: 3): Changing rate expectations affect loan demand, asset quality, deposit pricing, and operating costs. Although SoFi describes its balance sheet as liability sensitive, actual rate paths and deposit beta assumptions can alter net interest income and credit performance.
- **Credit and collateral risk** (Severity: 4): Home equity loans are subordinate-lien and structurally riskier. Property value declines, rising rates, or weaker economic conditions could increase delinquencies, defaults, and losses, with limited GSE salability.
- **Operating expense growth and margin pressure** (Severity: 3): Sales and marketing, technology, cost of operations, and G&A expenses have grown 20%-41% year over year. If revenue growth slows, operating leverage reverses and net margin misses terminal assumptions.
- **Loan Platform Business performance history gap** (Severity: 3): The Loan Platform Business is new and lacks significant performance history. If third-party loan demand or fee rates decline, a growing source of fee revenue and capital-efficient growth could reverse.
- **Product metric comparability risk** (Severity: 2): SoFi changed its SoFi Invest product metric to four products from two in Q1 2026 and did not recast prior periods. This can distort product growth comparisons and monitoring signals.
- **Guidance execution and valuation multiple compression** (Severity: 4): Management guidance implies high adjusted net revenue growth. If results fail to meet guidance, the market may lower terminal P/B or raise cost of equity assumptions, reducing expected return.
