Running a research: four flows

Same company (SoFi), same pipeline, same two human gates. Each variant is shown at the four moments that matter: starting, approving the plan, waiting while it runs, and the final review. Figures are illustrative.

Start is split in twoPick a company on one page, then a question and budget on another. No search over companies.
No idea what it costsBlank budget field, raw date-time cutoffs and a run-type dropdown, with no time or cost estimate.
Gates are easy to missThe plan gate only appears if you stay on the page while it polls. Nothing tells you to come back.
Running looks like an ops consoleEight cards: stage list, tasks, model calls table, activity log, backtest form. The story of the research is absent.
Review lives elsewhereGate 2 is a link to a separate page, so the run's context is lost at the decision.

My pick: B to start and decide, C to watch, D folded away

The research takes long enough that people leave, so the flow should be built around coming back: one-sentence start, gates delivered to "Waiting on you". When someone does open a run, show the report taking shape rather than a stage table. Keep D's phase track as the compact header and its operator tabs behind a "Details" toggle for you.

Cheap wins whichever you pick

  1. Time and cost estimate before Start, with a default cap.
  2. Cutoffs default to "now"; hide them behind Advanced.
  3. Group the 25 stages into 5 phases with the gates between.
  4. Gates show up in Home's "Waiting on you" with a count.
  5. Move model calls, tasks and backtests behind Details.
